05 / About

We built a school for the hands, and kept a notebook for the rest.

It started with a single bed of poor soil and a stubborn belief that the only way to learn craft was to slow it down. We dug, we recorded, and we returned the next season to read what we had written.

What began as a margin habit became a method. Each material we worked — loam, clay, dye — earned its own ledger, its own vocabulary, its own pace.

The first cohort arrived. Twelve makers, one long table, and a shared notebook that still sits in the studio today, dog-eared and entirely full.

Now a field school in earnest: three disciplines, a working farm of materials, and a single rule that has never changed — write before you work.
