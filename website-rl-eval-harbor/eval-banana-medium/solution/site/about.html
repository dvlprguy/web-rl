The Building
Has Opinions.

PROSE

We bought a kiln. A real one — fired bricks for ninety years, then sat empty for thirty. People told us to flatten it. We kept the walls and threw out everyone else's idea of comfort.

A hotel should not whisper. It should hold you. So we made rooms with weight: thick walls, low light, beds you sink into and resent leaving. We pointed the best ones west, because the sun is the only decorator we trust.

We don't upsell. We don't suggest the spa package. We tell you what a room costs and what it is. If that's too blunt, there are softer hotels. They will be delighted to see you.
